\subsection{Re-identifying cells over time}
\label{sec:reid}
On the \val{val.movies} held-out validation movies, re-identifying the same cell after 3 to
27 frames among all cells at that time (chance \val{re.chance}) separates the encoders
clearly (Figure~\ref{fig:benchmarks}). V-JEPA~2 is best (\val{re.E7_vjepa2_vitl}, 95\% CI
\val{re.E7_vjepa2_vitl.ci}), followed by the ImageNet ViT (\val{re.E2_imagenet_vit_s}) and
DINOv2 (\val{re.E3_dinov2_vit_s}); raw downsampled pixels reach \val{re.raw_pixels}
(\val{re.raw_pixels.ci}). TiJepa-1 reaches \val{re.E4_tijepa1} (\val{re.E4_tijepa1.ci}),
below raw pixels. Among TiJepa models the training objective matters: the spatial-only
ablation reaches only \val{re.E4s_tijepa1_spatial} (\val{re.E4s_tijepa1_spatial.ci}), and
randomly initialised networks \val{re.E6_random_init_draw0}--\val{re.E6_random_init_draw1}.
Shuffling the frames of TiJepa-1's input clips lowers its score to \val{re.E5_frame_shuffled},
so it uses the order of frames only slightly. Because this set is used to compare training
recipes, these numbers describe the models; they are not a held-out test.

\subsection{Associating cells across frames}
\label{sec:association}
With ground-truth cells in the six held-out movies, centroid distance alone matches
\val{as.position_only.pos.9} of cells correctly across a 9-frame gap (IDF1 over the movie
\val{as.position_only.pos.idf1}; Figure~\ref{fig:assoc}). Embeddings alone are worse for
every encoder: V-JEPA~2 \val{as.E7_vjepa2_vitl.emb.9}, TiJepa-1 \val{as.E4_tijepa1.emb.9},
DINOv2 \val{as.E3_dinov2_vit_s.emb.9}, random initialisation \val{as.E6_random_init_draw0.emb.9}.
Adding the embedding to position lowers accuracy for every encoder except V-JEPA~2, whose
combination (\val{as.E7_vjepa2_vitl.both.9}) is not distinguishable from position alone
(exact $p=\val{as.p.E7_vjepa2_vitl.both9}$); for TiJepa-1 it falls to
\val{as.E4_tijepa1.both.9} ($p=\val{as.p.E4_tijepa1.both9}$, the smallest possible with six
movies). Within the TiJepa family the ordering of the re-identification task recurs: on
embedding distance alone TiJepa-1 exceeds its frame-shuffled input by
\val{as.t1vs.E5_frame_shuffled.abs} and the spatial-only ablation by
\val{as.t1vs.E4s_tijepa1_spatial.abs} (both $p=\val{as.t1vs.E4s_tijepa1_spatial.p}$), and V-JEPA~2 exceeds TiJepa-1 by
\val{as.t1vs.E7_vjepa2_vitl.abs}. For densely packed migrating neutrophils at an
8\,s frame interval, position is the identity cue; appearance features of any model we
tested add nothing to it.

\subsection{Dimensional collapse}
\label{sec:collapse}
The effective rank of TiJepa-1's pooled embedding on a fixed set of microglia crops fell
from \val{mon.t1.rank.first} at step 14{,}751 to \val{mon.t1.rank.last} at the end of
training, and its per-dimension standard deviation from \val{mon.t1.std.first} to
\val{mon.t1.std.last}; the spatial-only ablation ended at \val{mon.ts.rank.last}
(Figure~\ref{fig:training}). The collapse is a property of the encoder, not of the probe
input: on held-out validation clips the pooled target-encoder and context-encoder embeddings
of TiJepa-1 have the same effective rank (\val{dg.E4_tijepa1.target.rank} and
\val{dg.E4_tijepa1.context.rank} of 384), the first principal component carries
\val{dg.E4_tijepa1.target.top1} of the variance and the first eight
\val{dg.E4_tijepa1.target.top8}. Individual tokens are less collapsed (effective rank
\val{dg.E4_tijepa1.token.rank}), so the information lost is mostly in what survives mean
pooling. TiJepa-0's pooled embedding has rank \val{dg.E0_tijepa0.target.rank} on the same
clips. Across the six pretrained and random networks, effective rank on the validation
clips is correlated with both the microglia accuracy (Spearman $\rho=\val{dg.rho.bb}$) and
the re-identification score ($\rho=\val{dg.rho.val}$), which is descriptive with six
models. In the training logs the rank of the training-batch embedding peaks within the first
20{,}000 steps and declines while the EMA momentum rises towards 1 and the learning rate
decays, so the cause cannot be separated from the schedule without changing the recipe;
Section~\ref{sec:recipe} reports a short recipe study that changes it.

\subsection{A short recipe study}
\label{sec:recipe}
To ask whether a change of recipe prevents the collapse, we trained five variants from
scratch for 20{,}000 steps each (schedules ending at 20{,}000 steps, snapshots every 5{,}000)
and scored every snapshot only on the validation set: the TiJepa-1 recipe (S0), EMA
momentum capped at 0.9995 (S1), a variance--covariance penalty on the predictor outputs
\citep{vicreg} (S2), an effective batch of 256 with a linearly scaled learning rate (S3),
and future targets drawn from long-stride clips with a deeper predictor (S4). Before
training we fixed the criterion for adopting a variant: a validation score at least 0.02
above S0 and at least twice its effective rank, both at 20{,}000 steps. No variant met it.
S0 reached \val{sw.S0.score} with effective rank \val{sw.S0.rank}; the variants changed the
score by \val{sw.S4.gain} to \val{sw.S2.gain} and the rank by a factor of \val{sw.S1.rr} to
\val{sw.S4.rr} (Figure~\ref{fig:training}). At 20{,}000 steps none of the runs had yet
collapsed (rank \val{sw.S1.rank}--\val{sw.S4.rank}, against \val{dg.E4_tijepa1.target.rank}
for TiJepa-1 after \val{pre.t1.steps} steps), so this budget cannot test the late-training
collapse; it shows only that none of these changes improves the early representation enough
to justify a full retraining.
