# Autofluorescence / FLIM Metabolic Imaging of Innate Immune Cell Activation States — Papers, Labs, and Public Data

Scope note on terminology used below: **"figures published"** = only plots/representative images in the paper; **"source data"** = numeric spreadsheets behind figures; **"raw/processed image data deposited"** = actual image files (e.g. Becker & Hickl `.sdt` TCSPC files, TIFFs, image patches) downloadable from a repository. I flag which applies in every entry.

---

## Q1. Which groups do FLIM / autofluorescence imaging of macrophage (and other innate immune cell) activation?

### Takeaway
At least a dozen groups do this. The densest bodies of work are **Melissa Skala (Morgridge/UW–Madison)**, **Alex Walsh (Texas A&M)**, **Michael Monaghan (Trinity College Dublin)**, **Maxim Darvin / Marius Kröger (Charité Berlin)**, **Timothy Muldoon (Univ. of Arkansas)**, **Irene Georgakoudi (Tufts)**, **Raluca Niesner / Ruth Leben (FU Berlin / DRFZ)**, and the **Gratton/Digman/Potma/Liu axis at UC Irvine** (the 2016 origin paper for label-free macrophage phenotyping). Kyle Quinn and Chiara Stringari are central to the *methods/consensus* side of optical metabolic imaging but I found **no** macrophage-activation-specific primary paper from either.

### Cited Findings — macrophage / monocyte activation

**UC Irvine (Gratton, Digman, Potma, Wendy Liu) — the origin paper**
- Alfonso-García A, Smith TD, Datta R, Luu TU, Gratton E, Potma EO, Liu WF. "Label-free identification of macrophage phenotype by fluorescence lifetime imaging microscopy." *J Biomed Opt* 2016;21(4):046005. PMCID PMC4833856. (Note: this is the paper everyone else cites as precedent; Alfonso-García was then at UC Irvine, later moved to Laura Marcu's lab at UC Davis.) — [Europe PMC record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/27086689); cited in the Kröger eLife reference list — [eLife 72819](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9581533/)
- I could **not** retrieve the full text of PMC4833856 (Europe PMC `fullTextXML` returned HTTP 500), so its donor counts, exact stimulation doses and data-availability wording are **unverified**. Flag for the writer.
- Related Digman-lab work on macrophage polarization uses *bioluminescence* phasor analysis rather than NAD(P)H FLIM: "Monitoring Macrophage Polarization with Gene Expression Reporters and Bioluminescence Phasor Analysis," *Chem Biomed Imaging* 2024, PMC11600157 — [PMC11600157](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11600157/)
- Digman lab also imaged macrophage NADPH-oxidase/superoxide responses to aerosols: Fang T, … Digman MA, Shiraiwa M, *Environ Sci Technol* 2022, PMC9730850 — [PMC9730850](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9730850/)

**Michael Monaghan lab (Trinity College Dublin) — the best-characterised M1/M2 2P-FLIM + ML study**
- Neto NGB, O'Rourke SA, Zhang M, Fitzgerald HK, Dunne A, Monaghan MG. "Non-invasive classification of macrophage polarisation by 2P-FLIM and machine learning." *eLife* 2022;11:e77373. PMC9578711 — [PMC9578711](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9578711/)
- Cells: **primary human monocyte-derived macrophages**, cultured 6 days, **6 donors**; stimulated **IFN-γ 20 ng/ml (M1) or IL-4 20 ng/ml (M2) for 24 h**, plus untreated control. Polarisation validated by flow cytometry (CD80, CD86 for M1; CD163, CD206 for M2), qPCR (CXCL9, CXCL10, MRC1, CCL13 vs 18S) and ELISA. — [PMC9578711](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9578711/)
- Instrument: custom **upright Olympus BX61WI** multiphoton system, pulsed **80 MHz Ti:sapphire (Chameleon Ultra, Coherent)**, **water-immersion 25×/1.05 NA (Olympus)**, 37 °C stage; **NAD(P)H excited at 760 nm, FAD at 800 nm**; emission bandpass **458/64 nm** (NAD(P)H) and **520/35 nm** (FAD); cells in ibidi Luer µ-slides, 0.8 mm channel. — [PMC9578711](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9578711/)
- Features: NAD(P)H τ1, τ2, τ_avg, α1/α2 (free vs bound fraction) plus optical redox ratio; real-time small-molecule perturbation **during** imaging; **FCCP** gave the strongest stratification.
- Classifier: **random forests**, three governing FLIM parameters, **ROC-AUC 0.944, out-of-bag error 16.67 %** for field-of-view-level classification; performance degraded at single-cell level because of donor variability. — [PMC9578711](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9578711/)
- Paired readouts: **Seahorse XF ECAR/OCR** (oligomycin, FCCP, rotenone+antimycin A, 2-DG; macrophages 2×10⁵/well, stimulated 24 h with 20 ng/ml IFN-γ or IL-4), ELISA, qPCR, flow cytometry. IFN-γ-M1 = higher ECAR / lower OCR; IL-4-M2 = lower ECAR / higher OCR. — [PMC9578711](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9578711/)
- **Data availability (exact wording):** *"All data generated or analysed during this study are included in the manuscript and supporting files (uploaded as source data)."* → **No repository deposit; source-data spreadsheets only, no raw FLIM images.** — [PMC9578711](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9578711/)
- Monaghan-lab FLIM has since been applied to rheumatoid-arthritis synovial tissue macrophages: Hanlon MM, … Neto NGB, … Monaghan MG, … Fearon U. "Loss of synovial tissue macrophage homeostasis precedes rheumatoid arthritis clinical onset." *Sci Adv* 2024, PMC11423874 — [PMC11423874](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11423874/)

**Maxim Darvin / Marius Kröger (Charité Universitätsmedizin Berlin) — in vivo human M1/M2, and the single best public macrophage FLIM dataset**
- Kröger M, Scheffel J, Shirshin EA, Schleusener J, Meinke MC, Lademann J, Maurer M, Darvin ME. "Label-free imaging of M1 and M2 macrophage phenotypes in the human dermis in vivo using two-photon excited FLIM." *eLife* 2022;11:e72819. PMC9581533 — [PMC9581533](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9581533/)
- Subjects: **25 healthy volunteers** (12 M / 13 F, 24–65 y, Fitzpatrick I–III), volar forearm, **6–12 tomograms per subject**, papillary dermis ≈70–130 µm depth; plus in vitro monocyte-derived M1/M2 MΦs, dermal MΦs isolated from skin, and ex vivo cryosections with CD68 (M1) / CD163 (M2) antibody ground truth. Ethics EA1/078/18, EA4/193/18, EA1/141/12. — [PMC9581533](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9581533/)
- Instrument: **DermaInspect two-photon tomograph (JenLab GmbH, Jena)**, tunable fs **Mai Tai XF (Spectra-Physics)**, 710–920 nm, 100 fs, 80 MHz; **760 nm excitation**; 3–5 mW in vitro/ex vivo, 40–50 mW in vivo; 6.8 s acquisition per image; TCSPC analysed in **SPCImage 8.0 (Becker & Hickl, Berlin)**; **bi-exponential** fitting, IRF typically <100 ps; axial/lateral resolution ≈1.2–2.0 / 0.5 µm. — [PMC9581533](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9581533/)
- Quantitative feature table (mean ± SD): in vitro monocyte-derived **M1** MΦs (n=21) τ_m **479 ± 106 ps**, τ1 163 ± 50 ps, τ2 1209 ± 161 ps, a1/a2 2.4 ± 0.6, TPE-AF intensity 600 ± 100 photons/mW; in vitro **M2** MΦs (n=27) τ_m **1185 ± 170 ps**. Table also covers dermal M1/M2 MΦs in vitro, ex vivo CD68/CD163 MΦs, in vivo MΦs, **monocytes, resting and activated human skin mast cells, dendritic cells, fibroblasts and neutrophils**. — [PMC9581533](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9581533/)
- Classifier: decision tree on a2 plus derived variables τ_m, τ2/τ1, a1/a2, (a1−a2)/(a1+a2). Ground truth (known-phenotype in vitro/ex vivo MΦs): sensitivity 0.95 ± 0.05, specificity 0.97 ± 0.06. MΦs vs other dermal cells: 0.81 ± 0.03 / 0.81 ± 0.03. **M1 vs M2+others: sensitivity 0.88 ± 0.04, specificity 0.89 ± 0.03; M2: 0.82 ± 0.03 / 0.90 ± 0.03.** — [PMC9581533](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9581533/)
- **Data availability (exact wording):** *"The data have been deposited in Dryad. The following dataset was generated: Kröger M, Darvin M. 2021. Macrophage FLIM raw data. Dryad Digital Repository."* — [PMC9581533](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9581533/)
- **Resolved accession: Dryad `doi:10.5061/dryad.8gtht76q2`**, titled "Label-free imaging of M1 and M2 macrophage phenotypes in the human dermis in vivo using two-photon excited FLIM," published **2022-11-01**, version 7, authors Kröger & Darvin (Charité), linked to primary article 10.7554/eLife.72819. Usage notes state: *"**.sdt files are a proprietary file format that can be analyzed with SPCImage software (Becker&Hickl, Berlin, Germany)**"* → **genuine raw TCSPC image data.** — [Dryad API record for doi:10.5061/dryad.8gtht76q2](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.8gtht76q2)
- Same group also published TPE-FLIM of **dermal mast cells** in healthy, allergy and mastocytosis subjects (*Sci Rep* 2020, PMC7484787) — [PMC7484787](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7484787/)

**Melissa Skala lab (Morgridge Institute for Research / UW–Madison) — largest overall portfolio**
- *3D tumour–macrophage microscale cultures:* Heaster TM, Humayun M, Yu J, Beebe DJ, Skala MC. *Cancer Res* 2020;80(23):5408–23. PMC7718391. Mouse **RAW 264.7** macrophages and human **THP-1** monocytes, 2D stimulation with **IFN-γ** or **IL-4 + IL-13**, NAD(P)H/FAD intensity + lifetime at **24, 48 and 72 h post-stimulation**; 3D microfluidic co-cultures with mouse PyVMT or primary human IDC breast cancer cells. Actively migrating monocyte-derived macrophages had higher redox ratio than passively migrating monocytes at 24 and 48 h. Population-density modelling of single-cell redox ratio revealed two sub-populations in every condition and time point. — [PMC7718391 (NCBI efetch full text)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7718391/)

- *Intravital mouse macrophages:* Heaster TM, Heaton AR, Sondel PM, Skala MC. "Intravital Metabolic Autofluorescence Imaging Captures Macrophage Heterogeneity Across Normal and Cancerous Tissue." *Front Bioeng Biotechnol* 2021;9:644648. **Irene Georgakoudi lab (Tufts)**

### Inferences
- The macrophage-activation FLIM literature converges on one reproducible signature: **M1/pro-inflammatory = shorter NAD(P)H mean lifetime and a higher free-NAD(P)H fraction (glycolytic); M2 = longer lifetime, more protein-bound NAD(P)H.** Absolute lifetime values are not comparable between studies — the Monaghan work excites FAD at 800 nm while Skala uses 890 nm, and Darvin's τ_m values (479 vs 1185 ps for M1 vs M2) are far apart from most other reports. The 2025 consensus-guidelines paper exists precisely because of this.
- The redox-ratio direction is **not** consistent across groups, partly because of definition (Georgakoudi uses FAD/[NAD(P)H+FAD]; Skala and Walsh use NAD(P)H/FAD or NAD(P)H/[NAD(P)H+FAD]), so a report writer must state the definition with any redox-ratio claim.

### Gaps
- Full text of Alfonso-García 2016 (PMC4833856), the foundational UC Irvine paper, could not be retrieved (Europe PMC 500 error); its protocol details and data statement remain unverified.
- Pixel dwell times are reported in only a minority of papers (I verified one: 4.8 µs in Schmitz 2025). Most macrophage papers do not state dwell time.
- No metabolomics-paired macrophage FLIM study found (the metabolomics pairings I found are in the neutrophil papers, below).

## Q3. Has FLIM / autofluorescence been applied to TRAINED (β-glucan, BCG, oxLDL) or TOLERANT (LPS-tolerized) monocytes/macrophages?

### Takeaway
**Partially — one study, and it uses a different trainer.** There is exactly one paper I found that applies NAD(P)H FLIM to a bona fide *trained immunity* protocol: **Carlile et al., *iScience* 2024, using heat-killed *Staphylococcus aureus* to train primary human monocyte-derived macrophages**. For the canonical trained-immunity stimuli in the assignment — **β-glucan, BCG and oxLDL — I found no FLIM or autofluorescence imaging study at all. And for LPS tolerance / endotoxin tolerance (prime–rest–restimulate), I found no FLIM or autofluorescence study at all.** Those are clean negative results. No trained/tolerant macrophage FLIM data are deposited anywhere.

### Cited Findings — the one positive
- Carlile SR, Cahill SC, O'Brien EC, **Neto NGB**, **Monaghan MG**, McLoughlin RM. "*Staphylococcus aureus* induced trained immunity in macrophages confers heterologous protection against gram-negative bacterial infection." *iScience* 2024;27:111239. PMC11607596. Labs: Rachel M. McLoughlin (lead contact, rachel.mcloughlin@tcd.ie) and Michael Monaghan, **Trinity College Dublin**. — [PMC11607596](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11607596/)
- Protocol (quoted/paraphrased from the figure legend): *"Human peripheral blood monocytes were isolated by CD14+ selection and exposed to heat-killed S. aureus strain Newman (**2 μg/ml**) or PBS in presence or absence of **2DG**. Media was refreshed after **24 h** and cells were maintained in RPMI supplemented with 10% pooled human serum. On **day 3 and day 7**, 2P-FLIM analysis was used to measure the average fluorescence lifetime of NADH (τ-avg) as an indicator of the amount of bound or unbound NADH."* — [PMC11607596](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11607596/)
- Result: *"2P-FLIM analysis revealed an increase in unbound NADH in S. aureus trained cells compared to PBS-treated macrophages on day 3 and day 7 post initial exposure to S. aureus … reflected in the significantly lower average lifetime fluorescence (τ-avg) in the S. aureus trained macrophages. A return almost to baseline was observed when macrophages were trained with S. aureus in the presence of the glycolytic inhibitor, 2-deoxy-D-glucose (2DG)."* Paired readout: **lactate release in supernatants**. — [PMC11607596](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11607596/)
- Imaging: same Monaghan-lab rig as the eLife M1/M2 paper — custom **upright Olympus BX61WI**, **80 MHz Chameleon Ultra (Coherent)**, **water-immersion 25×/1.05 NA Olympus**, 37 °C stage; **NAD(P)H 760 nm, FAD 800 nm**; **458/64** and **520/35** bandpass emission filters. (A separate phagocytosis assay used 860 nm excitation, analysed with CellProfiler.) — [PMC11607596](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11607596/)
- **Data and code availability (exact wording):** *"• Data reported in this paper will be shared by the lead contact upon request. • This study did not generate any new original data and code • Any additional information required to reanalyze the data reported in this paper is available from the lead contact upon request."* → **nothing deposited; note the internal contradiction between the two bullets.** — [PMC11607596](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11607596/)

### Cited Findings — the negatives
- A dedicated search for FLIM/NADH imaging of **β-glucan or BCG** training of monocytes returned **no imaging study**; only conventional immunometabolism papers. The canonical reference is non-imaging: β-glucan and BCG induce training via **dectin-1 and NOD2 → Akt–mTOR–HIF-1α**, with an **increased NAD⁺/NADH ratio and raised lactate** during training; β-glucan, BCG and oxLDL all drive a Warburg-type glycolytic switch, most pronounced with a **24 h training interval and 6-day rest**. — [Trained immunity as a novel therapeutic strategy, *Semin Immunol*/ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1471489217302084); [In Vitro Experimental Model of Trained Innate Immunity in Human Primary Monocytes, *Clin Vaccine Immunol*](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/cvi.00349-16)
- A dedicated search for **endotoxin-tolerance / LPS-tolerized** macrophage optical metabolic imaging returned **no study that images a prime–rest–restimulate LPS protocol**. The nearest designs are single-hit LPS studies: the POLG BMDM ± LPS FLIM study (Hu/West/Walsh) and the Kupffer-cell LPS+IFN-γ study (Beadell/Wong-Beringer). — [bioRxiv 2025.01.13.632822](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.01.13.632822v1.full); [PMC12607893](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12607893/)
- Of note, the recent **trained-immunity imaging-adjacent** literature uses transcriptomics, not optical metabolism: e.g. Earhart AP, Lopez AE, Hernandez JI, Nallapu A, Kulkarni HS. "The C3–C3aR axis modulates trained immunity in alveolar macrophages," *eLife* 2026, PMC13561512, which deposits **RNA-seq at GEO `GSE281001`** (and reuses `GSE282132`) with code at https://github.com/Kulkarni-Lung-Lab/Re-Analysis-BCG-airway-complement-scRNAseq — **no imaging data**. This is a useful contrast: the trained-immunity field deposits sequence data readily, but there is no imaging counterpart. — [PMC13561512](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13561512/)

### Inferences
- **The gap is real and specific.** All the biological ingredients exist — trained immunity is defined by a glycolytic/NAD⁺-NADH shift that NAD(P)H FLIM reads directly, Monaghan's group has already shown the readout works on day 3 and day 7 after a single training exposure, and the standard β-glucan protocol (24 h train, 6 day rest, restimulate) is exactly the longitudinal, non-destructive design that label-free imaging is best suited to. Nobody has done β-glucan, BCG, oxLDL or LPS-tolerance FLIM.
- Equally, **no trained- or tolerant-macrophage autofluorescence image data exist in any public repository**, so a new dataset would be the first of its kind.

### Gaps
- I cannot prove a universal negative from search alone; I searched Europe PMC (956 hits for the macrophage × autofluorescence/FLIM × NADH intersection, reviewed titles of the top 100 by relevance), plus targeted searches on "trained immunity"/"endotoxin tolerance"/"tolerized" × autofluorescence/FLIM/redox ratio (150 hits reviewed by title) and dedicated web searches. Conference abstracts (SPIE Photonics West, BiOS) and non-indexed preprints could contain unpublished attempts.

- Imaging: custom **Bruker Ultima** multiphoton; **100× oil, NA 1.45**; **SPC-150 TCSPC (Becker & Hickl)**; **Insight DS+ (Spectra-Physics)**, 80 MHz; **excitation 750 nm (NAD(P)H), 890 nm (FAD), 980 or 1040 nm (PerCP)**; emission **440/80** (NAD(P)H), **550/50** (NK) or **550/100** (B) for FAD, **690/50** (PerCP); **Hamamatsu H7422PA-40 GaAsP PMT**; power <10 mW at 750 nm, <20 mW at 890 nm; **270×270 µm at 256×256 px, 4.8 µs pixel dwell, 60 s integration**. — [PMC12006760](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12006760/)
- Features: **9 variables — NAD(P)H τ_m, τ1, τ2, α1; FAD τ_m, τ1, τ2, α1; quiescent-normalised optical redox ratio**; plus **phasor G and S at 80 MHz and 160 MHz**. — [PMC12006760](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12006760/)
- Classifier: **random forest (scikit-learn)**, 70/30 split for lifetime models. **B cells: AUC 0.98; accuracy 0.934 with 9 variables; phasor-based accuracy 0.939. NK cells: AUC 0.97 (top four variables); accuracy 0.926 with 9 variables; phasor-based accuracy 0.892** (phasor classifiers used a 50/50 split). — [PMC12006760](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12006760/)
- Paired readouts: **ELISA (IL-6 for B, IFN-γ for NK)**, glucose and lactate colorimetric/fluorometric assays (BioVision), and **CD69 status read by PerCP-anti-CD69 imaging** (not flow). Activated NK cells had lower NAD(P)H mean lifetime and more lactate production; **random forest reached 93 % accuracy for activation state referenced to CD69**. — [PMC12006760](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12006760/)
- **Data availability:** GitHub only — **https://github.com/skalalab/schmitz_r-lymphocyte_activation** (see Q2).

**Mixed PBMC / monocyte identification (Skala lab, 2026)**
- Riendeau JM, Hockerman L, Maly E, Samimi K, Skala MC. "Autofluorescence lifetime imaging resolves cell heterogeneity within peripheral blood mononuclear cells." *Biophotonics Discovery* 2026;3(3):035003. PMC13557457. OMI of PBMCs from **three human donors**, quiescent vs activated (PMA/ionomycin-type activation). **Quiescent vs activated PBMCs classified with 93 % accuracy at just 2 h post-stimulation; NK-cell identification within the mixture was weaker at 74 % accuracy.** ORR defined as NAD(P)H/[NAD(P)H+FAD]. — [PMC13557457](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13557457/); [SPIE](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/biophotonics-discovery/volume-3/issue-03/035003/Autofluorescence-lifetime-imaging-resolves-cell-heterogeneity-within-peripheral-blood-mononuclear/10.1117/1.BIOS.3.3.035003.full); [bioRxiv 2026.03.06.710224](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.06.710224v1.full)

**Dendritic cells**
- "Carbomer-based adjuvant elicits CD8 T-cell immunity by inducing a distinct metabolic state in cross-presenting dendritic cells." *PLoS Pathog* 2021, PMC7840022 (Walsh AJ and Skala MC co-authors). Quantified **optical redox ratio = NAD(P)H/(NAD(P)H+FAD)** in adjuvant (ADJ)-stimulated DCs by multiphoton microscopy: *"We discovered a significant drop in the redox ratio in ADJ-stimulated cells compared to unstimulated cells,"* cross-validated with a bioluminescence NADPH/NADP⁺ assay showing lower NADPH/NADP⁺. **Redox ratio only — no lifetime parameters reported for the DCs; no data deposit statement located.** — [PMC7840022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7840022/)
- Kröger/Darvin's in vitro table includes **dendritic cells** alongside monocytes, mast cells (resting and activated), fibroblasts and neutrophils, with τ1/τ2/τ_m/a1:a2 and TPE-AF intensity — the single best published reference table of innate-cell lifetime values, and it **is** backed by the Dryad `.sdt` deposit. — [PMC9581533](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9581533/); [Dryad 8gtht76q2](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.8gtht76q2)
- Murine **bone-marrow-derived DCs, macrophages and neutrophils** are in the Kreiß/Lemire Zenodo intensity dataset (Q2 item 2); the associated review notes Lemire et al. 2022 found **increased NADH and FAD autofluorescence in innate immune cells (macrophages, DCs, neutrophils) compared with adaptive cells (T, B cells), and in stimulated cells**, and that CD4⁺ T cells and neutrophils could be discriminated in mixed samples by NADH autofluorescence level. — [PMC11808199](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11808199/); [Zenodo 15657646](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15657646)
- Knab systematic review tally: **2 dendritic-cell papers of 83.** — [PMC12162169](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12162169/)

### Inferences
- The Skala-lab template (3 donors, ~1,000–1,200 single cells, 9 lifetime/redox features, random forest, AUC 0.97–0.98, cytokine + lactate + surface-marker ground truth) is now the de facto standard design for immune-activation autofluorescence classification, and has been executed for T cells (2021), B and NK cells (2025), neutrophils (2025) and mixed PBMCs (2026) — **but not for monocyte/macrophage activation state in that same rigorous donor-replicated form.** That is the clearest methodological gap adjacent to the trained-immunity question.
- Activation classification is fast: 15 min for neutrophils, 2 h for PBMCs, which is why longitudinal training/tolerance protocols are tractable.

### Gaps
- No DC activation-state FLIM study with lifetime parameters and a classifier exists, as far as I can tell.
- Neutrophil/NK papers do not report Seahorse; they use LC-MS metabolomics (neutrophils) and lactate/glucose assays (NK/B) instead.

## Q5. Any in vivo autofluorescence / FLIM of tissue macrophages with immune-state readouts?

### Takeaway
**Yes — three strong in vivo systems: human skin (Darvin/Charité, with a public raw dataset), mouse dermis and tumour (Skala), and zebrafish wounds (Skala/Huttenlocher).** Plus FLIm of plaque macrophages (Marcu), which is compositional rather than metabolic.

### Cited Findings
- **Human, in vivo:** Kröger et al., *eLife* 2022 — TPE-FLIM of M1 and M2 macrophages in the papillary dermis of **25 healthy volunteers**, with CD68/CD163 immunohistochemistry ground truth on matched cryosections and laser-burned fiducial labels (28×28 µm) to co-register label-free and stained images; phagocytosis also visualised in vivo. Machine-learning M1/M2 sensitivities 0.88/0.82. **Raw `.sdt` data public on Dryad.** — [PMC9581533](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9581533/); [Dryad 8gtht76q2](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.8gtht76q2)
- **Mouse, intravital:** Heaster et al., *Front Bioeng Biotechnol* 2021 — Csf1r-mCherry macrophages in ear dermis vs pancreatic flank tumour; TAMs more oxidised (lower redox ratio), shorter NAD(P)H and FAD mean lifetimes; dermal macrophages more heterogeneous in all three, matching CD206/CD86 immunofluorescence heterogeneity. Wavelength-mixing (750 + 1041 nm) three-fluorophore acquisition. — [PMC8093439](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8093439/)
- **Zebrafish, in vivo:** Miskolci et al., *eLife* 2022 — macrophage NAD(P)H/FAD lifetimes during wound response; glycolysis inhibition lowered NAD(P)H mean lifetime; metformin raised wound-macrophage redox ratio. Code at github.com/skalalab/zebrafish_flim. — [PMC8871371](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8871371/); [eLife 66080](https://elifesciences.org/articles/66080)
- **Human tissue ex vivo / clinical:** Hanlon et al., *Sci Adv* 2024 — synovial tissue macrophage homeostasis loss before RA onset, with Monaghan-lab FLIM. — [PMC11423874](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11423874/)
- **Intravascular FLIm (compositional, not metabolic):** Marcu group — in vivo macrophage detection in rabbit atherosclerosis by time-resolved laser-induced fluorescence (PMC2672099), and label-free quantification of plaque-progression biomarkers by intravascular FLIm in *JACC Cardiovasc Imaging* 2021 (PMC8116358). — [PMC2672099](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2672099/); [PMC8116358](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8116358/)
- **Validation caution** from within the field: Szulczewski JM, Yesilkoy F, Ulland TK, Bartels R, Millis BA, Boppart SA, Eliceiri KW. "To label or not: the need for validation in label-free imaging." *J Biomed Opt* 2024, PMC11660684. — [PMC11660684](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11660684/)

### Inferences
- Only the Charité human in vivo study pairs an in vivo immune-state readout with a public raw dataset. The two Skala in vivo macrophage studies are the richest methodologically (wavelength mixing, mCherry reporter co-registration) but neither deposited images.

### Gaps
- No in vivo FLIM study of tissue macrophages under a trained/tolerant immunological manipulation (BCG vaccination, β-glucan, sepsis-induced immunoparalysis).
- The POLG, Kupffer-cell and cryopreserved-macrophage items are preprints or papers whose data statements I could not locate; flag as unverified on availability.
