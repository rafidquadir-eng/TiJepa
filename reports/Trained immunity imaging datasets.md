# No public dataset images trained immunity

**Verdict first: no public imaging or video dataset of trained, tolerant, or innate-memory immune cells exists, so a self-supervised imaging model of innate activation states must be trained on data the requester generates.** A Europe PMC full-text search for `"trained immunity" AND ("BioImage Archive" OR "Image Data Resource" OR "S-BIAD" OR "idr00")` returns **hitCount = 0** ([Europe PMC REST](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=%22trained%20immunity%22%20AND%20(%22BioImage%20Archive%22%20OR%20%22Image%20Data%20Resource%22%20OR%20%22S-BIAD%22%20OR%20%22idr00%22)&format=json&pageSize=30)); the BioImage Archive holds **59 studies** matching macrophage/monocyte/neutrophil and **none** is a β-glucan, BCG, oxLDL, MDP or LPS-tolerance study ([BioImages API](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biostudies/api/v1/BioImages/search?query=macrophage+OR+monocyte+OR+neutrophil&pageSize=100)); and an enumeration of **all 147 IDR projects and 105 screens** found no innate-immune training study and no innate-immune time-lapse at all ([IDR projects](https://idr.openmicroscopy.org/api/v0/m/projects/?limit=600), [IDR screens](https://idr.openmicroscopy.org/api/v0/m/screens/?limit=600)). Two genuine partial exceptions soften that zero: **Carlile et al., *iScience* 2024** applied NAD(P)H 2P-FLIM to human monocyte-derived macrophages trained with heat-killed *Staphylococcus aureus* on days 3 and 7 — real training biology, real label-free imaging, but data only "shared by the lead contact upon request" ([PMC11607596](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11607596/)); and **BioImage Archive S-BIAD829**, "Trained immunity induced by high salt diet impedes stroke recovery," is the one deposited image set in the archive that carries the phrase *trained immunity* — 67 files, CC0 — but it is fixed mouse brain immunofluorescence, not live cells under a prime–rest–restimulate protocol ([S-BIAD829](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biostudies/api/v1/studies/S-BIAD829)). What *is* abundant is the other half of the problem: the trained-immunity omics canon is almost entirely open in GEO on matched donors, and ~290 GB of permissively licensed innate-immune video (neutrophil migration, microglial phagocytosis) exists for self-supervised pretraining. The practical consequence is a two-track plan: pretrain on public label-free immune video, build target signatures from public omics, and generate the imaging-plus-omics training/tolerance dataset in-house.

## The field deposits sequence data and withholds images, and that pattern is near-universal

The absence of trained-immunity imaging data is not a repository problem, it is a field-convention problem compounded by the fact that imaging is rarely the primary readout. Myeloid infection-biology and spatial-pathology groups deposit to BioImage Archive routinely — dozens of accessions exist for mycobacteria in macrophages (**S-BIAD1876**, **S-BIAD2093/2094/2096**, **S-BIAD2463**), phagocytosis (**S-BIAD4019**, **S-BIAD1871**), NETs (**S-BIAD849**), monocyte fate (**S-BIAD4111**), and in vivo macrophage tracking (**S-BIAD3375**) ([BioImages API](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biostudies/api/v1/BioImages/search?query=macrophage+OR+monocyte+OR+neutrophil&pageSize=100)). The trained-immunity field, by contrast, deposits GEO series with exemplary completeness and treats microscopy as illustration. The clearest single illustration is Daniel & Satpathy's *PNAS* 2026 chromosome-conformation study of IL-4-primed macrophages: it generated Hi-C, Micro Capture-C, ATAC-seq, RNA-seq and ChIP-seq for **H3K27ac, H3K4me1 and H3K4me3**, deposited all of it as **GEO GSE247946**, and performed **no microscopy whatsoever** ([PMC13598234](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13598234/fullTextXML)). The canonical β-glucan granulopoiesis paper (Kalafati/Chavakis, *Cell* 2020) likewise has **no confocal, intravital or other microscopy at all** — imaging is limited to flow cytometry — while depositing five GEO series and stating "Data are available upon request to the Lead Contact" ([PMC7599076](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7599076/fullTextXML)).

A methodological warning carried forward from the notes, because it will otherwise generate false positives: a generic EBI BioStudies search for "trained immunity" returns **~89,620 hits** with accessions like `S-EPMC6121175` and `S-EPMC9181299` ([BioStudies API](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biostudies/api/v1/search?query=trained+immunity&pageSize=50)). **`S-EPMC*` records are PubMed Central literature stubs, not image deposits.** Only `S-BIAD*`, `S-BSST*`, `EMPIAR-*` and `idr####` accessions are real image deposits. Any inventory built by searching BioStudies without a collection filter will appear to find thousands of trained-immunity "datasets" that are abstracts.

There is also no morphological-profiling precedent to inherit. A Europe PMC search for `("trained immunity" OR "innate immune memory" OR "endotoxin tolerance") AND ("high-content imaging" OR "high-content screening" OR "morphological profiling" OR "Cell Painting")` returned **45 hits, all off-topic** — abstract books, reviews, cystinosis, proton-therapy microglia, tendon-on-a-chip ([Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=(%22trained%20immunity%22%20OR%20%22innate%20immune%20memory%22%20OR%20%22endotoxin%20tolerance%22)%20AND%20(%22high-content%20imaging%22%20OR%20%22high-content%20screening%22%20OR%20%22morphological%20profiling%22%20OR%20%22Cell%20Painting%22)&format=json&pageSize=40&resultType=lite)). The only published morphology of trained cells is qualitative: β-glucan-primed monocytes become "large cells with a morphology consistent with macrophages" and LPS- or β-glucan-treated human cells "were generally larger than control cultured macrophages" ([*Front Immunol* 2018, PMC6284042](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6284042/)). Nobody has pointed Cell Painting, QPI, holotomography or FLIM at β-glucan, BCG, oxLDL or an LPS prime–rest–restimulate design. That is a clean, specific white space rather than a search failure, and it means a deposited dataset would be the first of its kind.

The pattern across the table is the argument. In every case where a formal data-availability statement could be read, the deposited object is sequencing and the images are either absent from the statement or folded into "on request." The Tay lab's Zenodo deposit of extracted NF-κB traces is the field's best-practice ceiling: derived single-cell time-series are shared, the terabyte-scale image stacks are not.

Three further negatives worth recording because they close off plausible hypotheses. For **NK memory there is no imaging study at all** — only general NK live-imaging methodology (microwell/nanowell arrays, TIMING, two-photon patrolling), despite the platforms being mature enough to recover full per-cell contact histories ([PMC3464457](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3464457/), [PMC4693004](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4693004/)). For **human BCG or β-glucan trial cohorts**, no imaging of cohort cells is deposited anywhere and no trial paper has microscopy as primary readout; the closest human-cohort imaging in the Netea orbit is clinical arterial-wall inflammation, not cellular microscopy ([van der Valk, *Circulation* 2016, PMC4995139](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=%22trained%20immunity%22%20AND%20(%22Oil%20Red%20O%22%20OR%20%22immunofluorescence%22%20OR%20%22confocal%22)%20AND%20AUTH%3A%22Netea%22&format=json&pageSize=40)). And **no human primary monocyte endotoxin-tolerance live-imaging study exists** — the two rigorous tolerance-imaging papers are mouse primary (Tay/Hoffmann) and mouse cell line (Palaga).

## Label-free and FLIM imaging of activation states is mature, but only one raw macrophage dataset is public

The label-free side of the problem is in much better shape, with one important caveat: it images *classical polarization*, not training. At least a dozen groups do FLIM or autofluorescence imaging of macrophage activation, with the densest portfolios at **Melissa Skala** (Morgridge/UW–Madison), **Alex Walsh** (Texas A&M), **Michael Monaghan** (Trinity College Dublin), **Maxim Darvin / Marius Kröger** (Charité), **Timothy Muldoon** (Arkansas), **Irene Georgakoudi** (Tufts), **Raluca Niesner / Ruth Leben** (FU Berlin/DRFZ) and the **Gratton/Digman/Potma/Liu** axis at UC Irvine that published the 2016 origin paper ([Alfonso-García et al., *J Biomed Opt* 2016, PMC4833856](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/27086689)). The literature converges on one reproducible signature — **M1/pro-inflammatory macrophages show shorter NAD(P)H mean lifetime and a higher free-NAD(P)H fraction (glycolytic); M2 shows longer lifetime and more protein-bound NAD(P)H** — but absolute values are not comparable between studies, which is why a field-wide consensus-guidelines paper now exists ([*J Biomed Opt* 2025, PMC12604823](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12604823/)). Redox-ratio *direction* is also inconsistent across groups because the definition differs: Georgakoudi uses FAD/[NAD(P)H+FAD], Skala and Walsh use NAD(P)H/FAD or NAD(P)H/[NAD(P)H+FAD]. Any redox claim must carry its definition.

The deposit situation is bleak but not empty. The field's de facto norm is **"code on GitHub, numbers in Excel, raw TCSPC on request"** — Riendeau et al.'s 2026 PBMC paper states it explicitly: *"Raw images are available from the corresponding authors upon reasonable request"* ([PMC13557457](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13557457/)). Against that, exactly one unambiguously raw human macrophage FLIM dataset is public: **Dryad `10.5061/dryad.8gtht76q2`**, the Kröger & Darvin M1/M2 human dermis study, whose usage notes confirm *".sdt files are a proprietary file format that can be analyzed with SPCImage software (Becker&Hickl, Berlin, Germany)"* — genuine Becker & Hickl TCSPC decays from **25 healthy volunteers**, 6–12 tomograms each, plus in vitro monocyte-derived M1/M2 macrophages and ex vivo CD68/CD163-validated cryosections ([Dryad record](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.8gtht76q2), [eLife paper PMC9581533](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9581533/)). Note the Dryad search pathology: searching the literal title "Macrophage FLIM raw data" returns **0** results, so the eLife statement looks like a dead reference unless you search by paper title ([Dryad API](https://datadryad.org/api/v2/search?q=macrophage%20fluorescence%20lifetime)).

Quantitative phase imaging is thinner still and mostly descriptive. No macrophage-polarization QPI/DHM/ODT paper exists from Popescu/Phi Optics, Park–Yaqoob–Dasari/Tomocube, Marquet, Kemper, or the Tolde/Rosel/Brabek group; a Europe PMC query across macrophage × QPI/DHM/holotomography/ODT × polarization/activation returned **304 hits** with only three on-target primary papers, all 2026 and all from nanomedicine or Chinese optics/TCM labs ([Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=macrophage+AND+%22quantitative+phase+imaging%22&format=json)). The closest thing to a label-free time-course of M0→M1 is a digital-holography study of RAW264.7 volume dynamics with a steep increase at 6–8 h, but it uses a single cell line, assumes a constant refractive index (n_cell 1.38), has no molecular validation of M1 identity, and **contains no data-availability statement at all** ([PMC13233025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13233025/)). Ptychography (Phasefocus Livecyte) macrophage-polarization work appears absent from the peer-reviewed literature entirely.

The most important structural finding in this stream is that **label-free macrophage-state classification is dominated by spectroscopic/mechanical/scattering feature vectors plus classical ML, not by deep learning on raw brightfield or phase images, and reported accuracy collapses once donor heterogeneity and more than two classes are admitted**: ~98% for same-donor hyperspectral reflectance, **85.1%** for M1-vs-M2 and **72.3%** for three-class light scattering, **75.8%** for six-class autofluorescence flow, ~71% for three-class phasor FLIM, and ROC-AUC 0.944 at field-of-view level but degraded single-cell performance for 2P-FLIM ([PMC5566322](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5566322/), [PMC9515641](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9515641/), [PMC10061362](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10061362/), [*Front Bioinform* 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bioinformatics/articles/10.3389/fbinf.2023.1210157/full), [PMC9578711](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9578711/)). That accuracy curve is the single most reusable quantitative pattern here: it sets the bar a self-supervised video model would have to beat, and it says donor variance, not class separability, is the binding constraint.

## Trained-immunity omics is open, matched-donor, and ready to use as the target signature

This is the stream where the public record is strong enough to carry the multi-omic half of the project without generating anything. Essentially the whole Netea/Stunnenberg/Logie canon is open in GEO, and critically, most papers deposited RNA-seq plus histone ChIP-seq (plus ATAC-seq, WGBS or methylation arrays) as SuperSeries generated on the **same primary human monocyte donors**, which means trained-versus-tolerant contrasts can be computed within-donor rather than across studies.

Two anchors matter most. **GSE58310** (Saeed et al., *Science* 2014, `n=64`, human) is a single series combining RNA-seq and histone ChIP-seq across naïve monocyte, monocyte-derived macrophage, **β-glucan-trained** and **LPS-tolerized** states, with the summary stating that "β-glucan trains innate immune cells through extensive remodeling of distal regulatory region-bound histone acetylation, resulting in a sizeable exclusive epigenomic signature" ([GSE58310](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE58310)). **GSE85246** (Novakovic et al., *Cell* 2016, `n=239`) is the densest single-experiment multi-omic trained/tolerant resource in human monocytes, explicitly stating that "ChIP-seq, RNA-seq, WGBS and ATAC-seq data were generated," with subseries **GSE85243** (RNA-seq, `n=94`), **GSE85245** (ChIP-seq, `n=132`) and **GSE87218** (ATAC-seq, `n=13`) ([GSE85246](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE85246)). On the tolerance side, the best counterpart is **GSE172116** (`n=99`, Knight lab), which profiles the coding and non-coding transcriptome, chromatin accessibility and epigenetic modifications in the same human macrophage acute-versus-endotoxin-tolerant design ([GSE172116](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE172116)).

For the human cohort layer, **300BCG is split-access**: processed ATAC-seq counts are open as **GEO GSE241092** (`n=848` samples, 323 individuals, day 0/14/90), raw ATAC-seq is controlled in **EGA EGAS50000000090**, and immune phenotyping including cytokine production capacity is open on **Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.10288920** ([300BCG portal](https://medical-epigenomics.org/papers/300bcg/), [GSE241092](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE241092)). Its headline biological result is directly relevant to any state model: BCG "enhances the innate immune response **only in individuals with dormant immune states at baseline**." A pipeline needing only accessibility matrices plus phenotypes can stay entirely open-access; only re-processing from raw reads needs an EGA application. **500FG/HFGP genotypes are controlled** (EGA dataset **EGAD00010002149**, DAC **EGAC00001002135**, 482 samples, signed Data Access Agreement required) and HFGP cytokine-QTL phenotypes are not in any open repository, so those must come from paper supplementary tables ([EGA](https://ega-archive.org/datasets/EGAD00010002149)).

**Two naming corrections from the notes must be carried forward.** First, **there is no "Mulder et al. Cell 2021 (300BCG)"**: the 300BCG flagship multi-omics paper is **Moorlag et al., *Immunity* 2024, PMID 38198850** (lead contact Christoph Bock, senior author Mihai Netea), while **Mulder K. et al., *Immunity* 2021, PMID 34331874** is a separate cross-tissue single-cell monocyte/macrophage atlas (MNP-VERSE, ~178,651 mononuclear phagocytes from 13 tissues across 41 datasets) with **no verifiable accession for the integrated object**. Conflating them is an error. Second, the **"Jeremy Jones"** named in the original brief appears to be a name error: no FLIM/autofluorescence immune-metabolism publications exist under that name, and the relevant Jones in this literature is **Isabel Jones**, a co-author on Skala-lab immune autofluorescence papers ([PMC12006760](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12006760/)).

## Immune video exists at scale, but never with an activation-state label and never with omics

For the self-supervised pretraining half of the project the public record is genuinely usable, with one hard limit: **no public dataset pairs immune-cell video with omics or with a per-cell molecular readout.** The notes searched for this specifically and found none.

The immune video ecosystem is bimodal. In vitro, the **ComplexEye** lineage out of Essen/ISAS Dortmund has produced the largest openly licensed single-cell-type immune video corpus anywhere: **186.8 GB across 21 ZIPs on Zenodo, CC BY 4.0**, raw brightfield movies of **primary human neutrophils** at **1 frame / 8 s**, mostly 1-hour recordings, **1280 × 800 px 8-bit grayscale** over an **825.8 × 512 µm** field (≈0.65 µm/px), from a study that tracked **>1.2 million neutrophils across 1,042 movies** and screened 1,000 compounds in 4 days ([Zenodo 7962144](https://zenodo.org/records/7962144), [PMC10713721](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10713721/)). The same lineage supplies curated supervision: **S-BIAD2196 "CellTrack"** holds raw human neutrophil brightfield time-lapse **plus instance segmentation masks with tracking information, label IDs consistent across time, all in OME-Zarr/OME-NGFF, CC BY 4.0**, and **S-BIAD2133 "AutoQC-Bench"** adds **>8,000 brightfield neutrophil-migration frames** from human and mouse neutrophils with manually annotated anomaly masks under an **MIT licence** ([S-BIAD2196](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biostudies/api/v1/studies/S-BIAD2196), [S-BIAD2133](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biostudies/api/v1/studies/S-BIAD2133)). In vivo, the Swiss lineage supplies point-level ground truth: **LTDB** (38 4D two-photon intravital videos, **728 consensus tracks / 44,722 centroid annotations**, 12–60 s frame interval, xy 0.27–1.15 µm, z 2–4 µm) and its successor **Immunemap** (**>400 videos, 58,943 tracks, 1,049,340 centroid annotations**, including **11,047 neutrophil** and **241 dendritic-cell** tracks, with a stated **CC0 waiver on the data**) ([PMC6049032](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6049032/), [PMC12759063](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12759063/)).

Two things do *not* exist that a reader might assume do. The classic computer-vision benchmarks are empty of immune cells: **none of the 10 Cell Tracking Challenge 2D+Time datasets involves immune or phagocytic cells**, and none of **CTMC-v1's 14 cell lines** is immune or phagocytic ([CTC](https://celltrackingchallenge.net/2d-datasets/), [CTMC](https://motchallenge.net/data/CTMC-v1/)). And **no public bead- or bacteria-phagocytosis *movie* dataset for macrophages** could be verified: **S-BIAD1871** has the ideal design (alveolar macrophages, 0.2 µm FluoSpheres or phage PAK_P1, **0/1/3/5 h**, ± 20 µM cytochalasin B as a built-in engulfment-blocked control, **CC0**) but the cells were **fixed before imaging**, so it is a timepoint series, not a movie ([S-BIAD1871](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biostudies/api/v1/studies/S-BIAD1871)).

The closest thing to what the requester actually wants — video with activation-state labels — is three datasets, and the distinction between per-cell and per-condition labelling is decisive. **Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.16319732** is the only dataset with genuinely per-cell, per-trajectory activation labels derived from a live reporter inside the movie: zebrafish macrophages in **Tg(mfap4:mCherry-F)ump6; Tg(tnfa:GFP-F)ump5** larvae, where M1 = tnfa:GFP-positive within 6 hpa, M2 = transitioned from M1 at 10.5–16 hpa, M0 = unwounded ([arXiv 2507.19314](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19314v1), [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/16319732)). Its construction is the template to copy, because it makes the activation label an **image channel** rather than metadata. Its two caveats are serious: the authors state **no specific M2 marker was available**, so M2 is inferred from reporter history plus a time window, and the licence is **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0** — the most restrictive in the whole catalogue. **Dryad 10.5061/dryad.m0cfxpp8v** gives real 5-minute-interval phase-contrast movies of murine BMDMs labelled M0/M1/M2, but at file level, not per cell ([Dryad](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.m0cfxpp8v)). **BBBC054** gives per-cell morphological phenotype labels (round/amoeboid/stratified) with (x, y, FOV) coordinates across an LPS exposure time course sampled every 30 min from 0–30 h, but each FOV is a different exposure time, so it supports classification along a time axis rather than tracking within a movie ([BBBC054](https://bbbc.broadinstitute.org/BBBC054)).

## Recommendations: reuse the omics, pretrain on neutrophils, generate the training dataset yourself

**Reusable off the shelf, immediately.** The entire multi-omic target-signature layer: **GSE58310** and the **GSE85246** family for within-donor β-glucan-trained versus LPS-tolerized contrasts with the exact histone marks requested; **GSE172116** as the tolerance counterpart; **GSE241092** plus **Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.10288920** for the 300BCG cohort without touching EGA; **GSE47189/GSE46903** for the M1/M2/spectrum anchor and **GSE118165** (DICE) when the signature must be chromatin-level and stimulation-aware; **GSE323311** for the crucial temporal fact that stimulus-specific information persists in chromatin at day 6 and is lost for some stimuli by day 11, which should set the imaging time points. For pretraining, the ~290 GB CC-BY-or-freer mix of ComplexEye, PhagoStat, S-BIAD2196, S-BIAD2133 and S-BIAD3674, with Immunemap and LTDB reserved as point-tracking evaluation sets. For transfer-learning initialisation on label-free immune morphology, **idr0109-zaritsky-melanoma** is the methodological precedent worth studying: public phase-contrast live-cell imaging with per-cell category labels and interpretable deep learning, which proves the archives accept exactly this data shape.

**Must be generated in-house.** Everything that makes the project novel: label-free or autofluorescence imaging of human monocytes/macrophages across naive, β-glucan-trained, BCG-trained, oxLDL-trained, LPS-tolerant and IFN-γ/IL-4-polarized states, with paired per-condition cytokines, RNA-seq, ATAC-seq and histone marks on the same donors. No public dataset pairs immune-cell video with omics or with a per-cell molecular readout, and no FLIM, autofluorescence, QPI, holotomography or Cell Painting study has ever been applied to β-glucan, BCG, oxLDL or a prime–rest–restimulate LPS design. One request is worth making before generating from scratch: **Boehringer Ingelheim's SImA3.1 macrophage Cell Painting collection** (iPSC-derived and primary MDMs, 4–5 donors, Opera Phenix 40×, LPS and IL-4+IL-13 controls, flow-validated) is explicitly email-accessible and is the best-characterised macrophage-polarization image collection in existence; whether access would actually be granted is untested.

**The closest template to copy is the Monaghan/McLoughlin pairing at Trinity College Dublin, extended with the Skala-lab design discipline.** Carlile et al. already executed the exact experiment one step removed from the target: they trained **primary human monocyte-derived macrophages** with a microbial stimulus, refreshed media at 24 h, and read NAD(P)H τ-avg by 2P-FLIM at **day 3 and day 7** post-training, recovering a glycolytic free-NADH shift that reverted toward baseline under 2DG and was corroborated by supernatant lactate ([PMC11607596](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11607596/)). Swapping heat-killed *S. aureus* for β-glucan, BCG and oxLDL, and adding an LPS prime–rest–restimulate arm, is a protocol substitution on an already-working rig (Olympus BX61WI, 25×/1.05 NA water, NAD(P)H 760 nm / FAD 800 nm, 458/64 and 520/35 emission). Three design elements should be imported from elsewhere. From the Skala lab's Schmitz et al. template — now executed for T, B, NK and neutrophil activation but conspicuously **never for monocyte/macrophage activation state in that rigorous donor-replicated form** — take the structure that works: 3+ donors, ~1,000–1,200 single cells per state, 9 lifetime/redox features plus phasor coordinates, random forest, and cytokine plus lactate plus surface-marker ground truth, which delivered AUC 0.97–0.98 ([PMC12006760](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12006760/)). From Kröger & Darvin, take the deposit standard: raw `.sdt` TCSPC files on Dryad with CD68/CD163 immunohistochemistry ground truth on matched sections, the only in vivo immune-state study anywhere backed by a public raw dataset. And from the zebrafish Zenodo 16319732 design, take the single best structural idea in this entire landscape: **make the activation label an image channel** — co-image a live reporter alongside a pan-myeloid channel so that per-cell state labels come from the movie itself rather than from well-level metadata.

Two practical warnings for the generation plan. Donor variance, not class separability, is the binding constraint — every published label-free classifier degrades from ~98% same-donor to 72–85% once donors and more than two classes are admitted, so donor count should be treated as the primary power parameter, not cell count. And Seahorse, cytokine and flow data have **no deposit convention at all** in this field; depositing them alongside the images would make the resulting dataset unusually valuable, and would be the first trained-immunity imaging deposit in any public archive.

## Conclusion

The sharpest finding is not that trained-immunity imaging data are scarce but that the two halves of the requester's problem live in non-communicating literatures with incompatible norms. The trained-immunity community is epigenomic by design — its reference human protocol (Bekkering 2016) produces cytokine supernatants and chromatin, not adherent cells on glass — and it deposits sequence data meticulously. The live-cell imaging that does exist on innate memory comes from systems-biology signaling-dynamics labs (Tay, Hoffmann, Palaga) that model LPS tolerance as a ligand-pair problem and never cite an image repository, and from optical-metabolism labs (Skala, Monaghan, Darvin, Walsh) that have built exactly the right readout for a glycolytic training shift and pointed it at M1/M2 instead. The biological ingredients for the missing dataset are all proven: training is defined by a NAD⁺/NADH and lactate shift that NAD(P)H FLIM reads directly; Monaghan's group has already shown the readout survives 3 and 7 days after a single training exposure; and the standard 24 h-train, 5–6 day-rest, restimulate protocol is precisely the longitudinal, non-destructive design that label-free imaging exists to serve.

That makes the gap an opportunity with an unusually short path. The target signatures are downloadable today, a ~290 GB permissively licensed corpus of brightfield innate-immune video is available for self-supervised pretraining, and the closest published protocol needs a reagent swap rather than a new method. The two things that should change in the plan as a result: first, budget donors rather than cells, because every label-free immune classifier in the literature breaks on donor heterogeneity and not on class geometry; second, design the deposit from the start — raw TCSPC or phase stacks plus the paired cytokine, RNA-seq, ATAC-seq and histone data, under CC BY or CC0, in BioImage Archive. On the evidence assembled here, that deposit would not merely be useful to the model. It would be the first of its kind in the field.
